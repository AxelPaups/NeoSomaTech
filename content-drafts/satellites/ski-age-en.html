Introduction: is there a minimum or maximum age for skiing with an exoskeleton?

It's one of the most common questions: from what age can you use a ski exoskeleton, and when is it too late (or too early)? The short answer: there is no official age limit. What matters is your weight, your build and your skiing level.

This article is part of our complete guide to the ski exoskeleton. It details the real criteria that decide whether an exoskeleton suits you or someone close to you.

 

Chapter 1: age isn't the right criterion, weight is

A passive spring-based exoskeleton like the Ski-Mojo is set according to your weight: the spring stiffness must match the load it has to take. In our guide to choosing the Blue, Silver or Gold spring, the softest spring (Blue) is for skiers of roughly 110 to 155 lb (50 to 70 kg), which often includes teenagers and people with a smaller frame.

In other words, a teenager of 110 lb and up may fall within the adjustment range, while a young child, too light and still growing, is outside the intended use of this kind of equipment.

 

Chapter 2: children and teenagers

This equipment is designed for adults or teenagers who have finished part of their growth. For a child, two points to watch:

  • Build: the fittings and straps are sized for an adult or teenage frame, not a child's.

  • Growth: a developing body generally doesn't need offloading, and medical advice is essential before any equipment of this kind.

If a teenager really suffers from knee pain while skiing, see a sports doctor or physiotherapist first: pain deserves a diagnosis before any gear solution.

 

Chapter 3: skiers over 50, 60 or 70

This is the age group most affected by joint pain, and the one for which the ski exoskeleton became popular. There is no upper age limit: what matters is your physical condition at the time, your ability to ski safely, and your doctor's opinion if you have a condition (arthritis, joint replacement, operated ligaments).

Over 60? Our article on exoskeletons and mobility after 60 covers safety and independence more broadly.

 

Chapter 4: how to know if an exoskeleton fits your case

  • Check your weight: it must fall within the product's adjustment range.

  • Check your skiing level: you should already ski independently, an exoskeleton doesn't teach you to ski.

  • If you have a knee condition: ask your doctor or orthopedist. This article is not medical advice.

  • Try before you buy if possible: some resorts and shops offer daily rental.

Frequently asked questions

From what age can you use a ski exoskeleton?

There is no official minimum age: weight and build are what count. Current models target adults and teenagers from around 110 lb (50 kg). For a child, this type of equipment isn't suitable.

Is there a maximum age?

No. Many users are over 60. The criterion is your ability to ski safely and your doctor's opinion if you have a condition.

Can my teenager wear a ski exoskeleton?

If their weight falls within the product's adjustment range, technically yes. But for knee pain, see a sports doctor first: a diagnosis comes before equipment.

Is a ski exoskeleton discouraged after knee surgery?

That's not ours to decide: get your surgeon's or physiotherapist's approval before resuming skiing, with or without an exoskeleton.
