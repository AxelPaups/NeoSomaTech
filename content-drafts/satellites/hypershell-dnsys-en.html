Introduction: two reference brands in motorized walking exoskeletons

Hypershell and Dnsys are two of the most visible brands in the motorized hip exoskeleton segment for walking and hiking. Both promise the same thing: less effort uphill and over long distances. But their ranges, prices and spec sheets differ noticeably.

This comparison relies on the specifications published by the manufacturers and reflected in our product pages. It is not a field test. For the broader context (why an exoskeleton for hiking, and for whom), see our hiking exoskeleton feature.

 

Chapter 1: the ranges at a glance

Hypershell offers four models, all with a 24-month warranty and CE, RoHS and FCC certification: the Go X (€999), the Pro X (€1,199), the Carbon X (€1,799) and the Ultra X (€1,999).

Dnsys offers the X1 series: the X1 Lite ($689), the X1 Carbon ($849) and the X1 Carbon Pro ($989), with a 1-year warranty and CE certification. Bundles with Z1 knee modules (SingleJoint, DualJoint) also exist, from $1,598 to $2,198.

Note: Hypershell prices are in euros and Dnsys prices in US dollars, so they can't be compared directly without conversion.

Chapter 3: what these numbers say

  • Tight budget: the entry-level Dnsys (X1 Lite, $689) and the Hypershell Go X (€999) are the two entry points. Dnsys is cheaper upfront, while Hypershell offers a warranty twice as long (24 months vs 1 year).

  • Long-distance range: Hypershell's Ultra X claims 30 km, the longest range in this table. The other models sit between 15 and 20 km.

  • Weight: Hypershell's Carbon X and Ultra X (1.8 kg) are the lightest, ahead of Dnsys's X1 (1.9 kg). The gap is small: at this level, wearing comfort matters as much as the listed weight.

  • Expandability: Dnsys offers Z1 knee modules to add to the X1 base, which the Hypershell range doesn't provide in our product pages.

Range and assistance are manufacturer-stated values measured under their test conditions: they vary with terrain, elevation gain and your weight.

 

Chapter 4: which one for your profile?

  • Occasional hiker, controlled budget: Dnsys X1 Lite or Hypershell Go X.

  • Big days and long distances: Hypershell Ultra X for its stated range.

  • Need knee support on top of hip assistance: the Dnsys X1 base with a Z1 module.

Frequently asked questions

Hypershell or Dnsys: which is better?

There's no absolute winner. Dnsys is cheaper at entry level; Hypershell offers a longer warranty and the model with the longest stated range (Ultra X, 30 km). The right choice depends on your budget and the distance you cover.

What is the price difference between Hypershell and Dnsys?

Prices are in different currencies: €999 to €1,999 for Hypershell, $689 to $989 for the Dnsys X1 models (excluding bundles with knee modules). Convert before comparing.

Are these exoskeletons suitable for seniors?

They can help you walk longer, but ask your doctor if you have a joint or heart condition. See our article on exoskeletons after 60.

Can you try one before buying?

Check directly with the manufacturers or resellers about their trial and return conditions before buying.
