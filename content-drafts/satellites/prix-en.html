Introduction: from $190 to over $4,000, why such a gap?

"How much does an exoskeleton cost?" has no single answer. Depending on whether you mean a support legging for skiing, a spring-based exoskeleton, a motorized walking model or professional back-support gear, the price runs from under $200 to over $4,000.

The ranges below are based on the prices shown in our catalog at the time of writing. They can change: always check the current price on the product page. To pick your product family before looking at budget, see our general exoskeleton comparison for 2026.

Chapter 2: what drives the price up

  • Motorization: motor, sensors, software and battery cost more than a spring or a technical fabric. The jump from passive to active is the first factor in the gap.

  • Materials: carbon fiber and light alloys (titanium, aluminum) reduce weight and raise the price, as the gap between a Hypershell Go X (€999) and an Ultra X (€1,999) shows.

  • Specialization: professional equipment built for one precise movement (arms overhead, head raised, bending forward) is made in small runs, so it costs more.

  • Warranty: some brands offer 24 months, others 1 year: at a similar price, this matters.

 

Chapter 3: costs to plan on top of the purchase price

  • Choosing the right version: for a spring-based exoskeleton, the spring is chosen by your weight (see our Blue, Silver or Gold guide): better to get the right setting from the start.

  • Shipping and taxes: for products sold in dollars or shipped from abroad, shipping and customs fees may apply. Ask the seller before ordering.

  • Accessories and upkeep: spare battery, straps, wear parts depending on the model.

 

Chapter 4: reimbursement, funding and alternatives

Sport or comfort exoskeletons are generally not reimbursed by health insurance. Some prescribed medical devices may be partially covered: ask your doctor and your insurer. For professional equipment, funding for musculoskeletal disorder prevention sometimes exists for employers: ask your occupational-risk insurer or your occupational health physician.

If budget is the obstacle, two options: try a support garment before an exoskeleton, or, for skiing, try a model on daily rental where a shop offers it.

 

Frequently asked questions

How much does an exoskeleton cost in 2026?

Expect under $200 for a support garment and over $4,000 for specialized professional equipment. A spring-based ski exoskeleton sits around €700, and a motorized walking model runs from about $700 to $2,400 depending on brand and range.

What is the cheapest exoskeleton?

In our catalog, textile support garments (from €192) are the entry level. They don't provide the same assistance as a mechanical or motorized exoskeleton.

Is a motorized exoskeleton worth the price?

It depends on your use. For long days of walking over hilly terrain, motorized assistance can justify the gap; for occasional use or skiing, a passive model is often enough. See our active vs passive comparison.

Is an exoskeleton covered by health insurance?

Generally not for sport or comfort models. Only some prescribed medical devices may be partially covered: check with your doctor and your insurer.
